# Series bible: Lessons from Market History (investing)

Promise: one true story per episode, 3-5 minutes, 4:3, ending with 2-4 principles you can use for life.
Tone: calm, curious, a little wry. Never hype. Principles, not picks.

## Compliance (non-negotiable)

- Disclaimer on every episode: "Educational content only, not financial advice." (lint enforces it.)
- No tickers to buy or sell, no price targets, no predictions, no performance promises, no "guaranteed".
- Historical prices: say "about" and cite; mark charts "approximate" when built from several sources.
- Quotes from famous investors: use the exact wording and the year/source (a shareholder letter, a book).
  Popular misattributions (Newton's "madness of people", Einstein on compound interest) are labelled as such.
- Real people are shown as typographic medallions, never generated likenesses; no imitated voices.

## Episode ideas (history + person)

1. The Genius and the Bubble: Newton & the South Sea Company, 1720 ✔ (lessons/investing/001)
2. Tulip Mania: what really happened in 1637 (the myth is bigger than the bubble)
3. The first stock: the Dutch East India Company, 1602 (what owning a business means)
4. Mr. Market: Benjamin Graham's imaginary partner (1949)
5. The fund that was mocked: John Bogle and the first index fund (1975-76)
6. When Genius Failed: LTCM, 1998 (leverage)
7. The Boy Plunger: Jesse Livermore's fortunes and ruin (rules vs. emotions)
8. The Psychology of Human Misjudgment: Charlie Munger's 1995 talk (inversion)
9. Losses loom larger: Kahneman & Tversky (behaviour)
10. 1929 and 1987: two crashes, two recoveries (time horizon)

Each episode: one story, one misconception, 2-4 principles (at least one reused from earlier episodes so the
series builds a vocabulary), one retrieval question, next-episode tease.
